Working Together to Strengthen Human Judgment in the Age of AI

Trilateral Human–AI Research Collaborative (THARC)

A U.S.–UK–Australia Community for Human-Centered AI Research, Education, and Ethical Decision-Making

Artificial intelligence is changing how people make consequential decisions in engineering, education, healthcare, infrastructure, and other high-stakes settings. The central challenge is not only whether AI can offer a useful recommendation, but whether a human–AI team can reach a well-informed decision while preserving human judgment, accountability, and the ability to question, verify, modify, reject, or override AI advice.

THARC brings together faculty and researchers across the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia to study and practice responsible human–AI collaboration. We co-develop shared scenarios, deliberation protocols, and knowledge resources so that AI organizes evidence, stakeholders, risks, and alternatives — while the person remains responsible for the final decision.

THARC is an independent academic collaboration. It is not endorsed by, affiliated with, or speaking for the Fulbright Program, AUKUS governments, or any defense agency.

Invitation

Why Join THARC?

Participation is designed to be valuable for established and junior faculty alike. You can begin with a bounded contribution and grow into papers, book chapters, cross-country studies, or future proposal teams.

  • Access to a knowledge-grounded deliberation agent and shared benchmark scenarios
  • Support for co-developing discipline-specific scenario variants with a common protocol
  • Shared instruments, rubrics, and technical assistance for classroom or community studies
  • Cross-country collaborators in engineering ethics, AI/computing, psychology/human factors, education, and responsible AI
  • Opportunities to contribute approved data, annotations, and qualitative interpretation
  • Pathways toward joint publications, an interdisciplinary edited-book effort, and follow-on proposals
  • Differentiated roles so you are not required to run a full study to participate meaningfully
  • A welcoming entry point for junior faculty seeking international collaboration with manageable first steps

You do not need to be an AI expert to join. Educators and researchers who care about ethical reasoning, professional responsibility, and human judgment are essential to this community.

Method at a glance

How the Research Works

A shared protocol makes human–AI deliberation observable before, during, and after AI support, so we can study verification, disagreement, revision, rejection, and override — not only final answers.

Unaided initial judgment

Participants respond without AI: decision, reasoning, stakeholders, evidence, uncertainty, confidence, and responsibility.

AI-supported deliberation

A knowledge-grounded agent retrieves relevant considerations and asks targeted Socratic questions rather than issuing a moral verdict.

Human challenge & evaluation

People may request evidence, disagree, verify, revise, reject, or override suggestions. Acceptance and resistance are part of the record.

Final accountable judgment

The participant makes and justifies the final decision. Responsibility stays with the human.

AI considerations in the collaboration

Organize evidence & alternatives Surface overlooked stakeholders Preserve provenance Invite challenge & override No automated moral verdict

Human retains judgment: AI may strengthen deliberation, but it does not replace professional or ethical responsibility. Majority opinion and model output are never treated as ethical truth.

Evidence ecosystem

Data and Community Perspectives

THARC combines complementary data channels so comparative classroom evidence can sit alongside professional knowledge and broader community perspectives — with clear provenance rules.

Reference & professional knowledge

Codes, policies, standards, research, published cases, and validated scenarios seed a reference layer that changes only when justified by update, review, or new authoritative sources.

Comparative classroom data

Common scenarios across U.S., UK, and Australian sites provide the primary comparative dataset on reasoning, reliance, challenge, and override behavior (subject to approvals).

Faculty & expert contributions

Disciplinary variants, annotations, and interpretation broaden the scenario library while preserving a shared measurement schema.

Community & public perspectives

Website contributions, workshops, and permitted public-discourse themes can expand an empirical/community layer. These are contextual perspectives — not representative national samples or ethical ground truth.

Frequency is not treated as correctness. New community data can inform questions and scenarios, but ethical and professional reference knowledge is updated through separate, accountable processes.
